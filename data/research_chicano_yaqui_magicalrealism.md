### Key concepts to look up in these sources:
- **huya ania** — wilderness world / natural world (where the Deer Singer lives)
- **yoania** — flower world / dream world (spiritual counterpart to physical world)
- **Surem** — the little people, pre-Christian Yaquis who became animals/nature
- **pahko** — ceremonial fiesta structure (maso = deer, pascola = clown-dancer)
- **Four directions** — cosmological orientation in ceremony

## WHAT TO READ FIRST (Priority Order for Ch.01 Decision)

1. **Pascua Yaqui Tribe official site** — governance awareness. Know what the consultation gate is protecting before you decide anything about the ceremony content.

2. **"Aztlán and Viet Nam" (Mariscal)** — your manuscript lives inside this tradition. Read the introduction at minimum. It will tell you what has already been done in Chicano Vietnam literature and where your manuscript is positioned.

3. **Silko's "Ceremony" analysis (Academia.edu)** — the closest structural parallel to George's liberation arc. Indigenous ceremony healing a veteran. Different tribe, same architecture. See how Silko handles the ceremony vs. narration vs. dialogue balance.

4. **Sacred Texts Yaqui Myths (Giddings)** — specifically the Surem legends and the deer dance sections. huya ania and yoania are the two cosmological concepts missing from Ch.01 that the workbook flags as SE candidates.

5. **GSU Thesis on Magical Realism in Chicano Literature** — craft-level. How the genre handles the boundary between realistic and mystical registers without collapsing either.
