## 3 · The contemporary war canon — context

The competitive frame is *the modern English-language war literature canon*,
broadly recognised by Pulitzer / National Book Award / NBCC / military-fiction
prize lists and university curricula. The taxonomy below is sketched only to
locate Ramos's positioning — none of these titles have an Omega score
(that's a proprietary internal metric), so direct numeric comparison is
deliberately avoided.

| Cluster | Representative titles | Recognition pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Vietnam, US literary canon** | Tim O'Brien — *The Things They Carried*; Michael Herr — *Dispatches*; Karl Marlantes — *Matterhorn*; Tobias Wolff — *In Pharaoh's Army* | NBCC / NBA / decades-long syllabus presence |
| **Vietnam, "from the other side"** | Bao Ninh — *The Sorrow of War* | International + translated; canon-adjacent |
| **Iraq / Afghanistan literary** | Phil Klay — *Redeployment* (NBA); Kevin Powers — *The Yellow Birds* | Contemporary canon; 2010s syllabus |
| **Chicano / Latino war fiction** | Rolando Hinojosa — *Korean Love Songs* / *The Useless Servants*; Charley Trujillo — *Soldados*; Daniel Cano — *Shifting Loyalties* | Recognized within Chicano-studies syllabi; **thin trade-market footprint** |
| **Classical war canon** | Remarque — *All Quiet on the Western Front*; Heller — *Catch-22*; Mailer — *The Naked and the Dead*; Crane — *The Red Badge of Courage* | Reference frame, not competitive set |

**The structural gap.** The Vietnam US-literary canon has almost no
Chicano-led titles at trade scale, even though Chicano / Latino service
representation in Vietnam was disproportionate. The Chicano-literary canon
has war titles but they have not crossed into the mainstream literary canon.
**The two have not been written by the same book.** That is the lane.
