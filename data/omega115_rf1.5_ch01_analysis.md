### INJECTION A — SEA ANIA / FLOWER WORLD

**Insertion point:** After the deer dancers emerge from the dark with antlers, before they complete their movement to the fire.

**Purpose:** Names and physically grounds the sea ania (flower world beneath the dawn where the dead dwell as flowers). Connects the deer dancer's antlers — which in authentic Yaqui ceremony carry red ribbons and flowers — to the fallen brothers' presence. Transforms the deer dancer from picturesque figure to cosmological carrier.

**Full prose:**

> Each dancer's antlers carried flowers — not decoration, not ornament. Red ribbons and blossoms wound into the bone, bright against the December dark, the color of something alive that had decided to stay that way. George's sensor filed them with the same neutrality it filed the copal smoke and the exit routes: *data.* But his chest registered what his tactical mind would not yet name. These were not the flowers of a parade. In Yaqui understanding — the Chieftain would tell him this later, speaking slowly in the Spanish that was not his first language — the flowers are where the dead dwell. Sea ania. The flower world, beneath the dawn, beneath the place the sun opens each morning like a wound that heals: this is where they go. Not darkness. Flowers. The sea ania is not a metaphor for the afterlife; it is the afterlife's actual address. And the deer dancer, carrying flowers into the firelight, carries the dead with him — brings them across the threshold between worlds the way a messenger crosses a checkpoint, producing the correct credentials, entering the light. George felt Rodriguez before he saw him. Felt the particular weight of him, the way a man occupies space even when he's gone, especially then.
