**Location:** Early in the chapter, before the first action beat — Ramos orienting himself

**INSERT:**
> He had two languages and they did not agree on what was happening.
>
> In English: he was a soldier on a mission in a war. History.
>
> In Spanish — *en español* — he was *un hombre escribiendo la historia de lo que le hicieron a los suyos.* A man writing the story of what they did to his people. *La historia.* In Spanish the word for history and the word for story are the same word. He had always found this more honest than the English version, which separated the two as if facts and memory were different things.

## INSERTION 3 — Ch.38: The Historical Appendix
**Function:** EXPLICIT DEFINITION (name the weapon)
**Ω now:** 96.88 → target 99.0+
**Location:** Open the chapter — first paragraph, framing device for the entire appendix

**INSERT:**
> History, in English, is a noun. A thing that happened. Fixed. Archived. Something you look up.
>
> *La historia,* in Spanish, is the same word as *story.* Something being told. Something with a teller. Something that is still, in some sense, happening — because the telling is part of the event.
>
> Ramos had spent three years deciding which one he was writing. The answer, he had finally concluded, was: both, in that order. First you establish the facts — the dates, the DCAS numbers, the FOIA records, the names. That is history. Then you put a narrator in the room with them. That is *la historia.* The appendix you are reading is both. The distinction matters because one of them can be filed and forgotten. The other one follows you home.

**MRF note:** Full definition, both registers named side by side. This is the chapter where the technique becomes transparent — Ramos as archivist *and* witness.

**Function:** SEAL THE ARC (resolution)
**Ω now:** 104.88 → target 106.0+
**Location:** Near the final pages — Ramos completing his work, the last beat before the close

**INSERT:**
> He finished writing at 0340. The last page. The last calculation.
>
> He had set out to write history — to correct the record, close the gap between 349 and 3,741, give the dead their right count. He had done that. The numbers were in the appendix, sourced, verifiable, sealed.
>
> But somewhere between the first chapter and this one, *la historia* had happened on its own. The story had climbed inside the history and refused to leave. George Ramos was in it now — not as evidence but as witness, which was something the Pentagon's filing system had no category for.
>
> He thought of the word. *Historia.* History and story, indistinguishable.
>
> The math had always worked like this. The equation looked like it had one solution. Then you solved it and found out the solution was you.

**MRF note:** Completes the arc. "The equation looked like it had one solution. Then you solved it and found out the solution was you." — this is the mathematics thesis fully resolved. Historia = the manuscript you just read.
